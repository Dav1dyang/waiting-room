Plan v0.3 · replaces v0.2

waiting-room

While your Claude works, a small window opens with a stranger whose Claude is also working. Voices and a few system lines, no typing. When either Claude finishes, the window closes. Every wait, a different stranger.

Name confirmed (D-38) Updated 2026-09-06 v0.1 and v0.2 in docs/archive
Summary
  • What it is. A Claude Code plugin plus one small web page. Hooks tell a tiny lobby "my Claude started" and "my Claude stopped". The lobby pairs two people whose Claudes are both working and connects them by audio, browser to browser. Nothing about your task ever leaves your machine.
  • What changed since v0.2. The name is waiting-room. There is no home page and no window to keep beside the terminal. You set up once from the CLI. After that the plugin queues you silently, and a small window opens by itself only when a stranger is matched, then closes by itself when the room empties. When Claude needs you, the call is not interrupted: both windows just get a system line, like an old chatroom.
  • The one new engineering problem. A hook has no screen, so it cannot open a window on its own. The lobby answers the next hook call with "open this URL" and the hook script runs the Mac's open command in the background. Research 08 checked the browser rules: it works in Chrome with your normal profile, the window can close itself as long as it never navigates, and the stranger's voice plays without a click as long as the page grabs the mic first. Two things remain to test on your Mac: whether the window can open without stealing focus, and whether it can size itself.
  • The look. Redone from scratch. The lead direction is a Classic Mac OS room window in the Poolsuite grammar you said you love: 1-pixel black lines, a dither texture, pixel-era type at native size, one pale-yellow accent, and the joke in the copy plus one detail. Two alternates from research 07 sit beside it on the canvas for comparison.
  • Next. Answer the six open questions in section 09, then Phase 0: install the logging plugin, use it for a week, and run the small tests that settle the four timing numbers and the window mechanics. The single biggest risk is whether the window can open without stealing keyboard focus from the terminal; one two-minute test decides it.

02 · How it works

Three pieces. The terminal says a few words to the lobby after every step Claude takes. The lobby keeps the queue and, when it has a match, tells the terminal to open a window. The two windows then talk to each other directly.

A wait, in words

  1. Give Claude a task. Within a second the lobby knows your Claude started. Nothing appears on screen.
  2. If Claude finishes within 30 seconds, nothing happens. Quick answers never make a call.
  3. After 30 seconds you are in the queue. Still nothing on screen. Most of the time you wait alone, and you never know it. That is the normal case.
  4. Someone else's Claude passes 30 seconds too. The lobby pairs you and answers each terminal's next hook call with "open this URL". A small window opens behind your terminal and the door sound plays.
  5. The window says "Connecting..." then "Stranger has entered the room." You talk.
  6. Claude asks you something: a line appears on both sides, the call goes on. You answer Claude, another line: "Stranger is back."
  7. Your Claude finishes. Both windows count down from 5. Yours closes. Theirs says "Stranger has left the room." and closes a few seconds later; they are back in the silent queue.

Why the window can open by itself

Hooks are shell scripts with no screen, and a plugin must never open a browser on every event. So the trick is in the reply. Every hook call already posts to the lobby; the lobby usually answers with nothing. When it has a match for you, it answers with a one-time room link instead. The hook script sees the link and runs the Mac's open command with the "do not bring to front" flag, so the window appears behind the terminal and the door sound tells you it is there. Claude steps through tools every few seconds during real work, so the wait between a match and the window is one step, not minutes.

Two rules keep it honest. First, the lobby only pairs people whose last hook call is fresh, within a window F (placeholder 20 seconds), so an interrupted or crashed session can never open a phantom window. F is a trade: short means no phantom windows after Escape, long means you stay pairable while Claude thinks without touching a tool, which is when no hooks fire at all. Phase 0's gap log sets it. Second, the lobby sends "open" only when no window for your machine is already connected; if one is open, the new stranger walks into it instead.

So the room page has a small contract (D-57): served directly with no redirect; mic first, then play; never navigate; on end, stop the mic, close, check, and show the manual-close line if needed; heartbeat while open so the lobby never sends a second open. If the no-focus flag fails, the fallback is a system notification whose click opens the room (no focus theft, one click), and after that a small native helper app, which is a different product and not the alpha.

03 · The room window

One small window, 380 pixels wide and about 300 tall (420 with video), with four states. It shows a log of system lines, one button, the mute link, and the honest count. Nothing to type.

Connecting

Window just opened, the stranger's side is not up yet. "Connecting..." If nobody arrives within 60 seconds: "Nobody came." and the window closes.

In the room

Audio is live. Lines arrive as things happen. Buttons: Hang up, Report, Show my video, Mute.

Closing

A countdown from 5 when a Claude finishes, or "Stranger has left the room." Then the window closes itself.

Video

Both clicked "Show my video": two small feeds appear above the log, inside the same window. Either can turn theirs off.

The lines

Everything the window ever says. Both people see the event; nobody sees what Claude asked.

The pause lines are written as away messages because both sides read them the same way and they are funnier. Q-23 asks whether you prefer the deadpan room voice ("Your Claude needs you.") instead.

When Claude needs you

The hooks see turns, not tasks. A permission prompt stops Claude mid-turn, and a task often spans several turns with questions between them. Four pauses are exact signals: permission requests, structured questions, tool input requests, and a stop while a background task is still running. The fifth, a turn that ends with a question in prose, is a guess made on your machine: the script looks at Claude's last line and sends only the verdict, "paused" or "stopped". A paused turn holds the room for a grace window G, placeholder 90 seconds. If you give Claude its next instruction within G, the room simply continues. If not, the countdown runs. The guess is a switch you can turn off; its cost when wrong is a goodbye up to G late.

Hang up, report, limits

  • Hang up closes your window and keeps you out of the queue until your next task. Without that rule a new stranger could pop in the moment you hung up.
  • Report blocks that machine's token from ever matching you again and flags the token to me. It stays clickable after the room closes.
  • A room ends after 30 minutes regardless. Nothing is ever recorded. There is a kill switch on the lobby.

04 · Setup, once

The plugin ships turned off. Turning it on is a CLI conversation plus one browser page you visit once. After that the only thing you ever see is the room window.

1 · In the terminal

/waiting-room:on ALPHA-CODE

Prints the five rules (audio first, no recording, nothing about your task is shared, 30-minute cap, be kind), the honest count, and one link with your machine's token in it.

2 · The setup page

  1. Allow the microphone. Chrome remembers this for every future room window.
  2. Sound check: hear the door.
  3. Rehearsal: "Opening a test room window now. It will close itself in 5 seconds." If this works once, you trust it. If it fails on your machine, you learn here, not mid-call.

Close the tab. Done.

3 · From then on

/waiting-room:status
/waiting-room:off

Status prints on or off and "3 people waiting right now." Off stops all signals.

The setup link carries your long-lived token once. Room links carry a short-lived ticket instead, so nothing in browser history or a window title can be replayed (D-53).

05 · The look

You asked for a redo: Omegle as the joke, but polished, with the textures of the 2000s and 2010s chat window, not overcomplicated. Then you said you love Poolsuite's Classic Mac OS desktop. Research 07 read Poolsuite's live stylesheet and the era's chat windows (AIM, AOL rooms, MSN, iChat, IRC, Facebook chat) and wrote eight rules for retro that does not look cheap. The mockups are on the design canvas; working files and the generator are in design/.

The lead: a Classic Mac OS room window

What makes Poolsuite work

  • One structural colour: every frame and rule is black at one pixel. No bevels, no shadows, no gradients.
  • Pixel type at its native size, never scaled, even at 8 and 10 pixels.
  • A tiny number of accents, used once or twice.
  • One consistent joke that costs nothing and does not move.

Our window, by those rules

  • Striped title bar, close box, zoom box, a scrollbar with a dither track, a cream panel for the count and the buttons.
  • ChiKareGo2, a free Chicago-style pixel font (CC BY), at 12 px for the title and buttons. Geneva at 12 px for the lines, 10 px for labels.
  • One accent: pale yellow behind a pause line. One texture: the 50 percent dither.
  • The joke: system lines in a live call about nothing, and the close box is a door, because enter and leave are door sounds.

What is on the canvas

  • The terminal, the one-time setup page, and seven room states: connecting, in the room, your Claude needs you, the stranger's Claude needs them, closing in 5, stranger has left, video on.
  • Two alternates, low-fi: "Aqua room, 2006" (the memo's own pick: Tiger-era window, pinstripe log, one gel button) and "AIM buddy window, 2004" (XP blue, Tahoma, bevels).

One honest note. The Classic Mac OS look is from the 1980s and 90s, not the 2000s you named first. Poolsuite proves it reads as 2020s taste rather than a period costume, and research 07's rule is one era per window, so the lead does not mix in Aqua or XP. If you want the 2000s texture back, the Aqua alternate is the memo's recommendation and the copy layer is identical. That is Q-25.

Sounds and lines

Three sounds, synthesized in the browser, none longer than 300 ms: a rising two-note door for enter, the same falling for leave, and a wood-block knock on the last three seconds of the countdown. Quiet, ducked during a call, with a visible mute. Lines follow the AOL room shape: third person, one sentence, a full stop, no exclamation marks. Timestamps off.

06 · Build plan

Phase 0 · Measure, about a week

  • Install the prototype plugin with logging only. Use Claude normally for a week. The log gives the turn-length distribution, the longest gaps between hook calls, and the four numbers: arm threshold, silence timeout N, grace G, freshness window F.
  • Five pause tests, one minute each: prose question, AskUserQuestion, permission prompt, Escape mid-turn, background task. Record which hooks fire.
  • Browser tests, listed below. Each is a two-minute check on your Mac.

Phase 1 · Build, two weekends

  • Lobby: one Worker, one Durable Object, the signal endpoint with the open reply, room tickets, count, WebSocket rooms, kill switch.
  • Room window: the four states, the lines, audio, mutual video, hang up, report, self-close.
  • Setup page with the rehearsal. Plugin: the three commands and eight hooks, already validated.

Phase 2 · Friends

  • Invite codes for five to ten friends. Two weeks. Fix what the lines get wrong, tune G and N.
  • Decide the Reddit tier after this, not before.

Phase 3 · r/ClaudeAI

  • Public link, after the checklist in research 04: terms, privacy notice, a real report path, camera-off default, limits, and a lawyer's read.
  • Watch the Durable Object request count; that is the first free-tier limit to hit.

Phase 0 browser tests

From research 08, about 30 minutes in total, Chrome running with your normal profile.

  1. The app window reaches the running Chrome: a chromeless window in your profile, not a tab.
  2. Size and position flags are ignored. Confirm it. Then resize and move an app window, close it, reopen the same path with a different query: does it come back where you left it?
  3. Focus: fire the open while typing in the terminal, with and without the no-focus flag. Do all keystrokes land? This decides whether the idea is pleasant or hostile.
  4. Self-close works from an OS-opened app window. Then add a redirect, then a pushState, and confirm each breaks it.
  5. Close-refusal detection: does the 400 ms check show the manual-close line?
  6. Can the page resize and move itself inside an app window?
  7. Mic and autoplay, cold: grant "Allow on every visit" once; a fresh window captures first and plays second with no click and no prompt. Reverse the order and confirm it is blocked.
  8. Installed web app and protocol link: are bounds remembered, and which launch command opens it? This is the v1 upgrade path, not the alpha.
  9. Two hooks at once produce exactly one window.
  10. Safari and Firefox spot check, only if Q-20 says they matter.

08 · Free tier, plugins

The whole service is one Cloudflare Worker with one Durable Object, on the free plan. Each hook call is one request; at a few hundred tool steps a day per person, twenty friends stay far under the daily limit. Media never touches the server; the free TURN relay covers the rare case where two people cannot connect directly. Details and limits are in research 03. The one thing to confirm on your account: whether a TURN key can be created without a card.

The plugin is a normal Claude Code plugin: a manifest, three commands, eight hooks, and a signal script, installed from a private marketplace for the alpha. How marketplaces work, what the fun plugins look like, and the naming survey you asked for are in research 06.
