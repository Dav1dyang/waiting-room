Plan v0.5 · replaces v0.4

waiting-room

While your Claude works, a small shaded window shows how many others are waiting too. When a stranger is matched it unrolls and you talk. When either Claude finishes, it closes. Every wait, a different stranger.

Name confirmed (D-38) Updated 2026-09-06 v0.1 to v0.3 in docs/archive
Summary
  • What it is. A Claude Code plugin plus one small web page. Hooks tell a tiny lobby "my Claude started" and "my Claude stopped". The lobby pairs two people whose Claudes are both working and connects them by audio, browser to browser. Nothing about your task ever leaves your machine.
  • What changed since v0.4. You answered the last three questions. Pool is the default tint, the lines are Geneva, and a stranger connects automatically: no pick-up click, because turning the plugin on is the consent. The Knock state is gone, a quiet room shades back up on its own, and the no-click audio test joins the focus test as a gate. TURN billing is settled: STUN only until a pair fails to connect, then a card with a cap we build ourselves. The threshold is 15 s now, not 30.
  • Two things to watch. A window on every long turn is fine only if it never steals keyboard focus from the terminal. And with no click, a fresh app window must turn the mic on and play the stranger's audio with no gesture. Two short Phase 0 tests decide; each has a fallback: the notification path, and the pick-up click.
  • The look. Unchanged in spirit: a Classic Mac OS window in the Poolsuite grammar, black at one pixel, pixel type at native size, one texture. New: pastel desktop tints chosen once at setup, a bar-graph audio meter like Poolsuite's radio, and the dot screen on video. Research 09 set the hexes and the icon grid; research 10 verified the dot screen and the shade. One more change from the research: picking up takes one click on each side, so the mic never turns on by itself.
  • Next. No questions open. Phase 0: install the logging plugin, use it for a week, and run the short tests that settle the four timing numbers and the window mechanics.

02 · How it works

Three pieces. The terminal says a few words to the lobby after every step Claude takes. The lobby keeps the queue and, once per task, tells the terminal to open the window. From then on the window and the lobby talk directly, and the two windows talk to each other.

A wait, in words

  1. Give Claude a task. Within a second the lobby knows your Claude started. Nothing appears.
  2. If Claude finishes within 15 seconds, nothing happens. Quick answers never make a window.
  3. At 15 seconds the lobby answers your terminal's next hook call with "open". A shaded window appears behind the terminal, silently: a title bar and "2 others are waiting for their Claude." Most of the time it says nobody else is waiting. That is the normal case, and now you can see it.
  4. Someone else's Claude passes 15 seconds too. The lobby pairs you. Both windows unroll, a notification knocks, the door plays, the mic turns on, and the log says "Stranger has entered the room." You talk. Nothing to click.
  5. Neither of you says anything for Q seconds: "Quiet room. Back in the queue." Both windows shade, mics off, and you are queued again.
  6. Claude asks you something: a line appears on both sides, the call goes on. You answer Claude, another line: "back".
  7. The stranger's Claude finishes first: their countdown shows on your side, "Stranger has left the room.", and your window shades back up. You are queued again, mic off.
  8. Your Claude finishes. Both windows count down from 5 with a knock on the last three. Yours closes. If you were alone, it just says "Your Claude is done." and closes.

Why this is simpler than v0.3

In v0.3 the lobby had to catch a match and slip "open" into the next hook reply at the right moment. Now the hook path does one thing: at 15 seconds it opens the window, once. Matching, lines, the count, the countdown, and shading all happen over the window's own connection, which is instant and does not depend on Claude touching a tool. The terminal side stays four words: started, stopped, paused, tick.

Three rules keep it honest. The lobby pairs only people whose last hook call is fresh (window F, measured in Phase 0), so an interrupted session is never matched. It sends "open" at most once per task, and if you close the window yourself nothing reopens until your next task. And a window whose Claude went quiet says so and closes itself after a short grace, rather than lingering.

03 · The room window

One small window, 380 pixels wide. Shaded it is 100 pixels tall, most of that title bars. Unrolled it is about 230, or 335 with video. Five lines of log, one row of buttons, the count line, two meters. Nothing to type.

Shaded (queued)

Title bar and the count line. Silent. This is what you see most of the time. Note: Chrome's own thin title bar sits above ours; the canvas draws only ours, so judge the double bar in Phase 0. Research 10 read the Chromium source: the page can resize its own window in a Chrome app window, with a 100 px floor. So shaded is 380 by 100 outer: Chrome's thin title bar, then ours, then the count line.

A stranger arrives

The window unrolls, a macOS notification knocks, the door plays, the mic turns on, the red dot appears. "Stranger has entered the room." Nothing to click.

Talking

Buttons: Hang up, Show video, Report, and the speaker toggle. Meters move only with real sound. Quiet for Q seconds and the room shades back up.

Closing

"Your Claude is done. Closing in 5." with a big 5 and a knock on 3, 2, 1. Then the window closes itself. If the stranger leaves instead, the window shades back up.

Video

Both clicked Show video: two small feeds above the log, color under a fine dot screen, in the same window. Either can hide theirs.

The lines

Everything the window ever says. Both people see the event; nobody sees what Claude asked.

When Claude needs you

The hooks see turns, not tasks. Four pauses are exact signals: permission requests, structured questions, tool input requests, and a stop while a background task is still running. The fifth, a turn that ends with a question in prose, is a guess made on your machine: the script looks at Claude's last line and sends only the verdict. A paused turn holds the room for a grace window G, placeholder 90 seconds. If you give Claude its next instruction within G, the room simply continues. The guess is a switch you can turn off.

Video, meter, sounds

  • The dot screen. Color video under a fine black dot grid, like Poolsuite's player. You asked for the simplest way without processing: a CSS overlay on the video element, a radial-gradient dot pattern at a 3 px pitch with multiply blending and a touch of contrast. Nothing is processed and nothing changes on the wire. Research 10 verified it: zero CPU, zero latency, nothing changes on the wire, and no blend mode (with black dots it is mathematically identical, and blend modes over video have real Chromium bugs). Poolsuite's own effect is the same idea, a one-bit 4 by 4 tile at 25 percent coverage over color video. A true 1-bit dither stays written down, unbuilt.
  • The meter. A row of thin ascending bars per speaker, lit from the left by the sound level, in the spirit of Poolsuite's radio meter. It is the one continuously moving thing in the window, and it moves only with real sound. Research 09 sized it: 28 bars at a 3 px pitch, 84 by 10 px, a loudness level rather than a spectrum, instant attack, 300 ms release, a peak-hold bar, unlit bars absent, a noise gate so silence is flat.
  • Sounds. Door in, door out, knock. Under 300 ms each, quiet, ducked during a call, a visible mute. The queued open is silent; the macOS notification is the knock; the door plays as the stranger enters. Turning the plugin on was the consent.

Hang up, report, limits

  • Hang up closes your window and keeps you out of the queue until your next task. So does closing the window yourself.
  • Report blocks that machine's token from ever matching you again and flags it to me. It stays clickable after the room empties.
  • A room ends after 30 minutes regardless. Nothing is ever recorded. There is a kill switch on the lobby.

04 · Setup, once

The plugin ships turned off. Turning it on is a CLI conversation plus one browser page you visit once. After that the only thing you ever see is the room window.

1 · In the terminal

/waiting-room:on ALPHA-CODE

Prints the five rules, the honest count, and one link with your machine's token in it.

2 · The setup page

  1. Allow the microphone, "on every visit". It turns on only when a stranger enters.
  2. Allow notifications: the knock when a stranger arrives, since the window opens behind your terminal.
  3. Sound check: hear the door.
  4. Rehearsal: click, then send Claude any message. A shaded test window opens behind the terminal and closes itself in 5 seconds, through the same path as the real thing. If it works once, you trust it.
  5. Pick your desktop tint. Pool, Shell, Mint, or Dusk.

Close the tab. Done.

3 · From then on

/waiting-room:status
/waiting-room:off

Status prints on or off and "2 others are waiting right now." Off stops all signals.

The setup link carries your long-lived token once. The room window's link keeps a constant path with a short-lived ticket in the query, so Chrome remembers its size and place and nothing in history can be replayed (D-53, D-58).

05 · The look

The Classic Mac OS lead is accepted. What follows are the four adjustments you asked for, informed by research 09 (Poolsuite's color system, icons, buttons, and what makes retro feel modern) and research 10 (the dot screen and the shade). Mockups are on the design canvas; working files and the generator are in design/.

Retro, and also modern and fun

Research 09 read Poolsuite's live stylesheet and store. The formula is not effects; it is a 1995 grammar laid out with 2020s whitespace. The rules we adopt:

  • Flat and generous. A window floats in a flat field of one pastel. No gradients, bevels, drop shadows, or desktop textures. Our desktop dither is gone.
  • Pixel type at native size, tightly tracked. ChiKareGo2 at 16 px with -1 px letter-spacing and line-height equal to the size. Every raster icon authored at 2x and shown at half, so it is crisp on a retina screen.
  • A 2 px grid. 1 px frames, 4 px button corners, 6 px window padding, 6 and 10 px gaps. Nothing looks approximate.
  • Color carries mood, never structure or meaning. Exactly two things mean something: the yellow highlight behind the one line that matters now, and the red dot that says the mic is live.
  • Motion is quantized. Stepped blinks, 0.1 to 0.15 s transitions, no transition on a button press, one 1 s halo on the live dot, nothing over 0.3 s.
  • The joke costs nothing and does not move. The close box is a door, the queued line wears a watch, and the count line tells the truth.

Colors: pastel tints, black lines

Everything structural stays black at one pixel on white or cream. Color lives in two places: the desktop behind the window, and one or two button fills. You choose the desktop tint once, on the setup page.

Black text is 11:1 or better on every one of these, more than double the accessibility bar. Dark mode later, as a token swap (same hues at a lower lightness), never as an inversion filter, which is what Poolsuite does and which turns pastels to mud.

Icons and buttons

Poolsuite keeps two icon registers and never mixes them: multi-color pixel art in the dock, strictly 1-bit black inside windows. We have no dock, so everything is 1-bit. Eight icons on an 11 by 11 grid, drawn on graph paper the way Susan Kare drew the first Mac icons: door (close), hang up, camera, flag (report), speaker on, speaker muted, person (the count), and a wristwatch (waiting, the Classic Mac wait signal), plus the shade widget in the title bar. Icons live in the title bar, the count line, the status strip, and the video panes. Buttons stay text only: three actions, one or two words each, and at 16 px a word is faster than a glyph.

  • Button anatomy, borrowed from Poolsuite's media buttons. A 1 px black border, 4 px corners, a 1 px ledge underneath and a 1 px white line inside the top edge. On press it drops one pixel into the ledge, no transition.
  • States in a 1-bit language. Hover: nothing. Disabled: dotted outline, grey text. The one action Return triggers wears the thick System 7 ring. Keyboard focus fills with the tint.
  • The frame reads double. Six pixels of cream between the window edge and the inner boxes, the way Poolsuite's windows are built.

What is on the canvas

  • The terminal, the setup page (mic, notifications, sound, rehearsal, tint), seven room states in the order of one wait: queued (shaded), a stranger arrived and talking, your Claude needs you, the stranger's Claude needs them, closing in 5, stranger left, video on with the dot screen; and the four tints side by side.
  • The two alternates from v2 are gone from the canvas; they remain in the artifact's "Mockup v2" version.

06 · Build plan

Phase 0 · Measure, about a week

  • Install the prototype plugin with logging only. Use Claude normally for a week. The log gives the turn-length distribution, the gaps between hook calls, and the four numbers: arm threshold, silence timeout N, grace G, freshness window F.
  • Five pause tests, one minute each: prose question, AskUserQuestion, permission prompt, Escape mid-turn, background task.
  • Browser tests, listed below. Tests 3 and 7 are gates. Focus decides whether the open-when-queued model survives; audio with no click decides whether automatic connect survives.

Phase 1 · Build, two weekends

  • Lobby: one Worker, one Durable Object, the signal endpoint with one open per task, room tickets, count, WebSocket rooms, quiet detection, kill switch.
  • Room window: shaded and unrolled states, the lines, audio with the meter, mutual video with the dot screen, hang up, report, self-close.
  • Setup page with the rehearsal and the tint picker. Plugin: three commands and eight hooks, already validated.

Phase 2 · Friends

  • Invite codes for five to ten friends. Two weeks. Fix what the lines get wrong, tune F, G, and N.
  • Decide the Reddit tier after this, not before.
  • The card: add it the first time two friends cannot connect (STUN only until then). Mint short-lived TURN credentials per room, set the relay budget, and the $1 budget alert. Before the Reddit post at the latest.

Phase 3 · r/ClaudeAI

  • Public link, after the checklist in research 04: terms, privacy notice, a real report path, camera-off default, limits, a lawyer's read.
  • Watch the Durable Object request count; that is the first free-tier limit to hit.

Phase 0 browser tests

From research 08 and 10, about 40 minutes in total, Chrome running with your normal profile.

  1. The app window reaches the running Chrome: a chromeless window in your profile, not a tab.
  2. Size flags are ignored; confirm it. Then resize and move an app window, close it, reopen the same path with a different query: does it come back where you left it?
  3. Focus. Fire the open while typing in the terminal, with and without the no-focus flag. Do all keystrokes land? If not, the fallback is the notification path, and D-62 is off the table.
  4. Self-close works from an OS-opened app window. Then add a redirect, then a pushState, and confirm each breaks it.
  5. Close-refusal detection: does the 400 ms check show the manual-close line?
  6. Can the page resize itself between 380 by 44 and 380 by 215 inside an app window? Research 10 says yes from the Chromium source, with a 100 px floor that macOS may raise to about 150; this test reads back the real outer and inner height and judges the double title bar.
  7. Audio with no click. Grant "Allow on every visit" once. In a fresh app window, three checks with no gesture: the mic captures; the stranger's stream plays through an audio element; a Web Audio tone plays. Expected: yes, yes, maybe. Fallbacks: the pick-up click (D-75) if the stream will not play; the notification's own system sound for the door if Web Audio is blocked.
  8. The dot-screen overlay over a live video element: correct blending, no dropped frames, survives a window resize. Research 10 gives the exact CSS; this only confirms it on your camera and lets you tune the pitch and dot strength live.
  9. The knock: a notification from the app window shows on macOS, and clicking it focuses the window.
  10. Two hooks at once produce exactly one window.
  11. Installed web app and protocol link, only as the v1 upgrade path.

08 · Free tier, plugins

The whole service is one Cloudflare Worker with one Durable Object, on the free plan. Each hook call is one request; at a few hundred tool steps a day per person, twenty friends stay far under the daily limit. The window's WebSocket uses the hibernating API so an open shaded window costs nothing while idle. Media never touches the server. Details in research 03.

TURN, the relay for the pairs that cannot connect directly, is the one place a card enters. Verified on Cloudflare's own pages: STUN is free and needs no key; TURN has 1,000 GB of free egress a month, then $0.05 per GB; budget alerts only send email, and nothing caps spend for you. Enabling Realtime needs a card on file, which the docs never state but a community thread and Cloudflare's billing rule both say. So: STUN only through Phase 1, no card. Add the card the first time two friends cannot connect in Phase 2, before the Reddit post at the latest. The cap is ours: short-lived TURN credentials minted only for a matched pair, a monthly relay budget in the lobby that stops minting at 500 GB, and a $1 budget alert as the backstop. Research 03's numbers stand: an audio-only alpha cannot get near 1,000 GB. Details in D-82.

The plugin is a normal Claude Code plugin: a manifest, three commands, eight hooks, and a signal script, installed from a private marketplace for the alpha. Marketplaces, fun plugins, and the naming survey are in research 06.
